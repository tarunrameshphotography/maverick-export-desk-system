# LinkedIn strategy

**Role:** the reputation engine. LinkedIn is where exporters, importers, bankers, CHAs, EPC officials and journalists decide who is credible.

| Decision | Recommendation | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary audience | MSME exporters and export managers; secondary: importers, multipliers, export-ready owners | They buy advisory and incentive services and they amplify |
| Primary account | Founder's profile. Company page reposts and runs the newsletter, charts and the Ledger. | Reach and accountability (Part 4) |
| Frequency | **Five posts a week (Mon–Fri) + the Saturday *Maverick Brief*** newsletter. Sunday off. Daily *research* regardless. | B2B weekend reach is low. A quality floor protects the brand. |
| Format mix | ~40% text + one chart or annotated document image; ~30% document/PDF carousels (5–9 pages); ~15% short founder video (60–120 s); ~15% text-only (Countdowns, Ask the Desk) | Documents and native media hold attention without an outbound link; charts carry data |
| Length | Signal posts 150–300 words; carousels up to 9 slides; newsletter 800–1,200 words | Long enough for fact → meaning → action |
| Links | Source link in the *first comment* (documents) or on the last carousel slide | Keeps the post native while still showing receipts |
| Commercial posts | At most 1 in 10. Always useful on its own. Disclosed if scrip-related. | Protects the Desk's credibility |
| Comments | The founder writes 3–5 substantive comments a day on others' trade posts, adding the fine print they missed | Comments are content: a low-cost way to be discovered by the right people |

Platform notes (dwell time and saves as signals, personal profiles outperforming pages, outbound links under-distributed) come from third-party 2026 analyses, not LinkedIn documentation. Treat them as hypotheses and test them against your own analytics in the first 60 days.


### How the system produces something interesting every day

Weekdays are **not** fixed themes. Each day's lead post is simply the **highest-scoring story** from the scoring model (Part 12). A light default rhythm follows real data calendars, so the Desk always has a fallback:

| Day | Default slot (used only if nothing scores higher) | Why that day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | *Countdown*: the week's dates that change exporters' economics | Planning mindset at week start |
| Tue–Wed | Best *Fine Print* or *Second Order* story | Monday–Tuesday notifications, and overnight US and EU publications, have landed |
| Thu | *Freight Pulse* (weekly container-rate read for Indian lanes) or *Draft Watch* | Drewry's World Container Index publishes on Thursdays |
| Fri | *Headline vs Reality* or *Ask the Desk* | Reflective; good for discussion |
| Sat | *The Maverick Brief* (newsletter) | Owners read longer pieces at weekends |

A backlog of 10–15 pre-researched evergreen-but-timely pieces (news-pegged explainers, Market Doors) means a quiet news day never forces filler.


### Educational content versus intelligence content


#### Intelligence

Triggered by a change. Time-bound. Answers "what changed, and what should I do?" Carries a source line and a date.


#### Education (news-pegged)

Triggered by a change, but explains the underlying mechanism: why BCD is the only duty a scrip can pay; how rules of origin work. It keeps its value for months and later feeds LEAP modules.


### Introducing LEAP without damaging credibility

1. **Weeks 1–6: no LEAP selling.** Build the Desk's record first.
2. **Weeks 4–8: show the curriculum being built from Desk material** ("this week's FEMA change becomes Module 6, Lesson 2"). Open a waitlist from the Saturday Brief only.
3. **Week 8+: launch the first online cohort** with a live session. Sell with specifics: the syllabus, the eight-week outcome, who it is *not* for.
4. **Offline reputation transfer:** short testimonials from past programme participants on camera (with consent); photos from real sessions; named partner institutions. No invented numbers.
