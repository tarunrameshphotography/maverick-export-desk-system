The central problem, solved first

Why would an exporter follow Maverick Minds?

Not because it is first with the news. Because it is first with the consequences.

Maverick Minds cannot out-speed Reuters or the Business Standard trade desk, and it cannot out-rank GTRI on national policy commentary. It doesn't need to. Every one of those sources stops at the headline. Nobody reliably does the next step: read the primary document, and work out what it does to a specific Indian product, destination market and manufacturing cluster, then say what an exporter should do this week.

That step is the gap, and it is wide. In 2026 alone, the rules an Indian exporter prices against changed many times over:

  • US tariffs on Indian goods went from 50% to a deal at 18%, then to a temporary 10% tariff, then to a Section 301 tier.
  • RoDTEP rates were halved and then restored within a month.
  • The Strait of Hormuz closed to routine shipping.
  • A new FEMA export regime takes effect on 1 October.

When the rules move this fast, evergreen "how to export" content goes stale quickly, and interpretation becomes the scarce thing.

Recommended positioning: The export intelligence desk for Indian MSMEs. Everyone reports the headline; we work out what it does to your order book, with the source document attached.

Ten decisions this document argues for

  1. Own consequences, not scoops. "What everyone else missed" becomes "the implication everyone else skipped". That claim is defensible every day. "We found hidden news" is not.
  2. Show receipts on every post. Each intelligence post names its primary source (the notification number, the Federal Register document, the ePing ID). This alone separates Maverick Minds from 90% of export content.
  3. Keep a public Calls Ledger. Log every forward-looking flag, revisit it on a set date, and grade it in public. No one in Indian export content does this. It turns claims into a track record.
  4. Put the founder first on LinkedIn. Mr. Keerthivhasan is the byline and the face, as the analyst, not a guru. The company page is the archive and home of the newsletter.
  5. Research daily, publish five days a week. Post five times a week, plus a Saturday brief, rather than seven thin posts. A quality floor beats a streak.
  6. Lean on four signature franchises for about 80% of output: The Fine Print, Second Order, Countdown and Headline vs Reality.
  7. Separate the scrip desk from the intelligence feed, and disclose. It is also a business with policy risk: RoDTEP's current extension ends on 30 SEP 2026 and its FY27 budget was cut 45%.
  8. Narrow LEAP. Build it for business owners who already make or trade something and want their first export shipment done properly. Don't build it for "side-income" seekers. Run it as a cohort, not a video library.
  9. Give Instagram a different job. It explains, in Tamil and English, and feeds LEAP. It never copies LinkedIn. WhatsApp becomes the owned channel, because that is where MSME owners actually read.
  10. Automate the research, never the judgement. Machines collect, deduplicate, triage, score and draft. A human picks the story, checks the claims and approves. Nothing auto-publishes.

Challenge · the scrip business

The scrip line depends on three things Maverick Minds doesn't control:

  • the scheme continuing past 30 SEP 2026
  • its budget, now cut 45%
  • importers' BCD bills, which FTAs are steadily shrinking

It should be run as one part of a broader Incentive Desk, covering ARR filing, realisation compliance, drawback and Export Promotion Mission support, not as the brand's commercial core. Part 4 covers why it should also be kept out of the intelligence feed.

Home turf

Maverick Minds is based in Coimbatore, according to its public website and the founder's public LinkedIn profile. That puts it inside one of India's densest export-manufacturing belts:

  • Tirupur knitwear, Karur home textiles and Erode turmeric
  • Coimbatore pumps, motors and castings
  • Namakkal eggs, Ambur and Vellore leather, and Tuticorin marine products

National intelligence voices are Delhi-centric and national in lens. A credible cluster lens ("what this means for Tirupur") is an asset Maverick Minds can own without pretending to be national on day one.

The five strategic risks

  1. Founder bandwidth. The founder cannot be researcher, writer, consultant, trainer and deal-maker. The research system (Parts 10–13) exists to take research and first drafts off his plate.
  2. Accuracy risk. An intelligence brand can be destroyed by one confident, wrong claim about a notification. The approval workflow (Part 15) is designed around this.
  3. Hype contagion. If LEAP's marketing borrows the "lakhs per month from exports" register of the mass-market trainers, it undercuts the intelligence brand.
  4. Policy dependency. See the scrip note above.
  5. Conflict of interest. Commenting on RoDTEP while earning on RoDTEP transfers is legitimate only with disclosure and separation.

Strategic patterns

  1. Education competes on scale and aspiration. That drives a race toward louder claims and lower prices. A premium, honest position is open at the top: "we teach the current reality, taught by people who work in it daily".
  2. Intelligence competes on national policy and is written for policymakers and journalists. Nobody translates it down to HS code, market, cluster and action.
  3. Everyone reacts to Indian announcements and big US headlines. Almost nobody mines foreign primary sources (ePing drafts, RASFF, FDA refusals, Federal Register, EU Official Journal) for Indian implications. That is where the "saw it first" moments actually come from.
  4. No one keeps score. A public ledger of calls is the single most differentiating trust device available.
  5. Regional depth exists only at beginner level. A cluster-aware intelligence voice is an empty slot.

Part 04

Positioning

Testing "finding what everyone else missed"

What works

  • It fits the name. "Maverick" earns meaning.
  • It is rare in the category.
  • It creates a reason to check the account daily.

What breaks

  • Taken literally, it's a speed race you lose to wires and GTRI.
  • It invites clickbait ("nobody is talking about this…").
  • It is unfalsifiable until the day you miss something, and then it's embarrassing.
  • Contrarian takes on policy are a reputational risk.

Opinion Keep the spirit and change the claim. Maverick Minds shouldn't claim to find hidden news. It should claim to find the hidden implication and the buried document. The headline is public; the second-order consequence for an Indian exporter usually isn't. That claim can be proved daily, and it survives an off day.

"Maverick" gets a precise, credible meaning: independent reading. In practice that means four behaviours:

  1. We read the primary source, not the press release about it.
  2. We say so when the fine print disagrees with the headline, including when the headline is the government's.
  3. We write our calls down and grade them in public.
  4. We say "we don't know yet" when we don't.

Positioning statement

For Indian MSME exporters and export-ready manufacturers who can't afford a trade-policy team, Maverick Minds is the export intelligence desk that reads the primary documents of global trade and works out what they mean for your product, your market and your next contract. News reports, trainers teach the basics and think-tanks advise governments. We turn signals into exporter decisions, with receipts.

Brand promise

Every week, at least one development that should change an Indian exporter's decisions. Sourced to the document, mapped to the product and market, with a clear "what to do".

Line options

  • Global trade, worked out for Indian exporters. (primary)
  • Before it's news, it's a notification. (intelligence franchise line)
  • Everyone reports the headline. We read the fine print.

Proof points to build

  • Source line on every post
  • The Calls Ledger
  • Public corrections
  • Cluster and HS specificity
  • Citations by EPCs, associations and media over time

Challenge · should scrip trading be separated?

Yes: in channel, in voice and with disclosure. But not in legal entity, for now.

  • "Maximum value! Immediate settlement!" rate posts next to a careful analysis of a DGFT notification make the analysis look like a sales funnel.
  • Any Desk post about RoDTEP/RoSCTL carries a one-line disclosure: "Disclosure: Maverick Minds facilitates RoDTEP/RoSCTL scrip transfers." Disclosing openly is itself a trust signal.
  • The Desk never makes calls on scheme continuation that could read as manipulating scrip prices. It reports the documents and the scenarios.

Founder or company?

Interpretation Founder-led, with the company as institution. Reasons:

  1. LinkedIn distribution strongly favours personal profiles over company pages. Third-party 2026 analyses consistently report several times the engagement. Treat the exact multiples as unverified, but the direction is not in dispute.
  2. Intelligence needs an accountable name. "Keerthivhasan says" can be held to account. "Maverick Minds says" is anonymous.
  3. Offline programmes already built trust in the person, and that trust transfers online through his face and voice.

The company page still matters. It is the archive, the newsletter's home, the place a banker or buyer checks, and the vehicle for posts that aren't opinion (data charts, the Ledger, event notes). As the team grows, add a second byline (e.g. a research analyst) so the Desk is visibly more than one person, which protects the brand from single-person dependency.

Brand voice guide

The character: a senior export manager who also reads the Gazette. Unhurried, specific, commercially minded. He explains the way you'd explain to a smart client across a desk, not to a classroom.

The uncertainty ladder

Uncertainty is stated with fixed words, so readers learn the scale:

  • Confirmed — in a notified document.
  • Reported — credible media, not yet notified.
  • Likely — our read, most evidence points one way.
  • Possible — a scenario worth preparing for.
  • Unclear — we don't know, and here is what would tell us.

Post anatomy (intelligence posts)

  1. Consequence line — who is affected, and how.
  2. What changed — two or three lines of fact.
  3. What it means — interpretation, labelled as such.
  4. Who should care — HS codes, markets, clusters.
  5. What to do this week — one to three actions.
  6. What we're watching — the next trigger, with a date if possible.
  7. Source line — document, number, date.

Corrections policy (part of the voice)

Mistakes get corrected in public: edit the post, add a first comment starting "Correction:", and log it in the Ledger. An intelligence brand that corrects itself visibly is more trusted, not less.

Challenge · should LEAP target a narrower audience?

Yes. The mass beginner market already has scaled incumbents with hundreds of thousands of followers, large student counts and aggressive pricing. Competing there means adopting their register, which contradicts the intelligence brand.

The underserved buyer is the owner of an existing business who has a product and wants the first export done right. They will pay more, finish the course, become exporters, and later need advisory and incentive services. They also generate scrips.

LEAP's promise should be concrete: "In eight weeks, go from 'my product could sell abroad' to a costed, compliant, buyer-ready export plan, with your first qualified buyer conversations under way." No income claims, no guaranteed buyers.

Keep "Earn" in the name, but define it as earning your first export order properly, never as an income promise.

The Angle Engine: eight questions that turn news into a Maverick Minds post

  1. Who pays and who gains? Follow the money through the value chain.
  2. Which HS lines? Name them. If you can't, the story isn't ready.
  3. Which Indian exposure? The value of India's exports of those lines to that market. Source it.
  4. Which competitor country moves? Relative change matters more than absolute change.
  5. Which cluster feels it first? Tirupur, Karur, Coimbatore, Ambur, Tuticorin…
  6. What's the deadline or window? When does it take effect, and when does it bite?
  7. What does the headline get wrong or leave out?
  8. What should an exporter do this week? If there's no action, it's a watchlist item, not a post.

Verification protocol

  1. Policy, tax, customs and scheme claims need the primary document open, not a summary of it. Record its number, date and URL.
  2. Numbers are copied from the source with the exact quoted text stored alongside them. Any derived number (%, growth) is recomputed by the analyst.
  3. Dates are checked for "effective from" versus "notified on", and converted to IST where relevant.
  4. Two-source rule for anything not in a primary document: two independent credible outlets, never two outlets repeating one wire story.
  5. Every sentence in the draft is tagged Fact, Interpretation, Forecast or Opinion in the claims table. The published post reflects those tags in its language (the uncertainty ladder).
  6. Pre-mortem line: "What would make this post wrong in a week?" If the answer is plausible, soften the claim or wait.
  7. Legal-interpretation items (duty liability, eligibility edge cases) get a quick check with a partner CA or customs broker before publishing.

Part 12

Content scoring model

The goal is not "find an export news article". It is find the story Maverick Minds can explain better than anyone today. So the model rewards angle and action as much as importance. It also penalises saturation.

Step 1: Gates (pass all four or discard)

  1. G1 Evidence: at least one primary source, or two independent credible secondaries.
  2. G2 Indian implication: an identifiable effect on Indian exporters or importers.
  3. G3 Not a repeat: we haven't covered it in the last 14 days, unless there is a genuine new development.
  4. G4 No unverifiable numbers: the core claim doesn't depend on a figure we can't source.

Step 2: Weighted criteria (each scored 0–5)

Under-coverage (inverse saturation)120–2 Indian items with this angle in 72 h10+ items with the same angle

Base score = Σ (weight × score ÷ 5), out of 100.
Final score = base × confidence multiplier (1.0 primary-verified; 0.85 credible secondary pending verification) − penalties.

Penalties:

  • −5 for geopolitical or political sensitivity.
  • −5 to −15 when the story needs speculation to be interesting.
  • −10 for a near-repeat.

Conflict of interest (scrip-related) is not penalised. It triggers a mandatory disclosure line instead.

Part 13

Automation architecture

Principle: automate collection, sorting and first drafts; keep a human on choice, truth and publication. Stages run in this order. The dashed stages are human gates.

  1. CollectCollectors pull feeds, APIs, page-change diffs and parsed email alerts (ePing, EPC circulars). Raw text is stored verbatim with a timestamp.
  2. Normalise & dedupeCanonical URLs, text hashes, then similarity clustering so 14 articles about one notification become one story.
  3. TriageA fast model classifies relevance and extracts entities: countries, HS codes, schemes, dates, clusters. Irrelevant items are dropped and logged.
  4. ScoreRules plus model fill in the Part 12 criteria. A saturation count comes from news queries. Ranked candidates.
  5. PickMorning Desk Sheet. A human chooses the lead and backup and adds field notes.
  6. Research & verifyA deep-analysis model with web search and fetch, restricted to trusted domains, builds a claims table: every claim carries a source URL, a quoted passage and a label.

  8. ApproveAnalyst edits; founder approves by risk tier (Part 15). Reason codes are captured on every edit and rejection.

Guardrails built into the pipeline

  • No claim without a quote. The structured output schema requires source_url and source_quote for every numeric, date or rule claim. Drafts citing a claim not in the table are rejected automatically.
  • "Unknown" is a valid answer. Prompts tell the model to leave a field empty rather than infer a rate, date or value.
  • Fetched pages are data, not instructions. Text from the web is passed as quoted content, and the model is told never to follow instructions found inside it. Verification fetches are domain-restricted.
  • Recency check. Every claim records the document date. Anything older than the latest amendment in the database is flagged "possibly superseded".
  • Disclosure trigger. Any story tagged RoDTEP, RoSCTL, scrip or duty credit automatically gets the disclosure line in its draft.
  • Repeat detection. Similarity search against the last 90 days of posts; near-duplicates are flagged before scoring.
  • Human approval required at stages 5 and 8. The system has no credentials to publish anything.

Why not auto-publish?

Opinion The brand's entire value is accuracy and judgement. One auto-published misreading of a customs notification would cost more trust than a year of daily posts earns. It also creates a quiet liability: exporters may act on it. The only thing worth considering for automation later is scheduling an already-approved post. Even the weekly Freight Pulse chart should get a 30-second human glance.

Pre-publish checklist (10 items)

  1. Primary source opened and its number, date and link recorded.
  2. Every number traceable to a quoted source line.
  3. "Effective" versus "notified" dates correct.
  4. Interpretation, forecast and opinion signalled in the language.
  5. HS lines, markets and clusters named correctly.
  6. An action is present, or the item is moved to the Watchlist.
  7. Disclosure line present if scrip, RoDTEP or RoSCTL is involved.
  8. No banned phrases; no income or outcome claims.
  9. Visual matches the text (no chart implying more precision than the data has).
  10. Any forward-looking call logged in the Calls Ledger with a review date.

Service levels and escalation

  • Lead post approved by 10:30 IST. If it isn't, the backup Green item runs, and nothing unapproved goes out.
  • Breaking primary-source news (e.g. a scheme extension notification) can use a fast lane: a factual-only post (what the document says, a link, "analysis tomorrow"), approved in 15 minutes.
  • Corrections: within 24 hours of detection. Edit the post, add a "Correction:" comment, and log a corrections row with its root cause. Review root causes monthly.
Part 16

Performance and learning loop

North-star metric: qualified conversations per month. These are inbound DMs, calls or forms from people who export, import or have a real product and an intent to export, attributed to content. Likes are a diagnostic, never a goal.

LayerMetricsHow to measure
ReputationQualified share of new followers and engagers; saves and sends per 1,000 impressions; reposts by credible accounts (EPC staff, association office-bearers, bankers, journalists); citations and invitations; lead time versus media coverageMonthly audit: sample 50 new engagers and classify them (exporter / importer / MSME / service provider / student / other)
HabitNewsletter subscribers and open rate; WhatsApp channel members; returning engagers (people who engage 3+ times a month)Newsletter and WhatsApp stats; engager log
PipelineDiagnostic bookings; LEAP waitlist and cohort sign-ups; advisory enquiries; Incentive Desk enquiries (sell and buy split)Lead form with "which post brought you here?"; UTM links; the leads table
QualityCorrection rate; Ledger hit rate (right / partly / wrong); claims failing verification at approvalDatabase
EfficiencyMinutes of founder time per post; time from document to postApprovals log

The loop

  1. Weekly (30 min, Friday). Top and bottom three posts by qualified engagement, not reach. For each, ask what in the franchise, angle, format or hook explains the result. Kill or keep one experiment.
  2. Monthly (90 min).
    • Audience-quality audit.
    • Ledger grading.
    • Score calibration: compare each post's predicted score with its realised qualified engagement and leads. If "Under-coverage" predicts outcomes better than "Time sensitivity", the learning job proposes shifting weight between them. A human accepts or rejects the change.
  3. Quarterly.
    • Revisit segments and franchises.
    • Retire any franchise below the median for two consecutive months.
    • Reconsider cadence.
    • Review the Incentive Desk against scheme developments.

Rules that stop the loop optimising the wrong thing

  • Never let a reach-winning format displace a franchise that produces leads.
  • Viral posts with a low qualified share are treated as a warning, not a win. They pull the audience off-target.
  • Compare posts only within their franchise. A Countdown and a Second Order do different jobs.

First-90-day targets (to calibrate, not to promise)

Opinion

  • At least 40% of sampled new engagers in target segments.
  • At least 4 Calls Ledger entries graded publicly.
  • At least 10 qualified conversations a month by day 90.
  • Correction rate under 1 in 30 posts.
  • A LEAP waitlist built only from the newsletter and WhatsApp.

Adjust after month two, once there's real baseline data.
